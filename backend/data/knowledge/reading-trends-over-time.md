# Reading biomarker trends over time

The core value of a longitudinal platform is that **trends beat snapshots**. A single reading is a point in time; a sequence of readings reveals direction, velocity, and pattern.

## What to extract from a trend

1. **Direction** — did the value rise, fall, or stay flat over the window?
2. **Magnitude** — the total change (absolute and percent) from first to last reading.
3. **Steepest step** — the single largest change between two consecutive readings; often the most decision-relevant event.
4. **Threshold crossings** — did any reading cross a clinical threshold (e.g. HbA1c ≥ 5.7%, LDL ≥ 160 mg/dL)?
5. **Rate** — is the change accelerating, steady, or decelerating?

## Grounding

- Every claim about a trend should reference **specific values and dates** (e.g. "LDL rose from 110 to 160 mg/dL, Jan 2024 → Jan 2025 — a 45% increase, with the steepest step +23 mg/dL between Apr and Jul 2024").
- No vague claims. No numbers that are not in the patient record.

## Practice

- Interpret the trajectory in the patient's **genetic context** (flags change how much weight a trend carries).
- Keep language clinician-facing; do not produce direct patient instructions.

# LDL cholesterol

LDL cholesterol ("low-density lipoprotein") is the main carrier of cholesterol in the blood and the primary target of lipid-lowering therapy.

## Units and ranges

- Measured in **mg/dL**.
- Common reference interpretation:
  - **< 100 mg/dL** — optimal / near-optimal.
  - **100–129 mg/dL** — above optimal.
  - **130–159 mg/dL** — borderline high.
  - **160–189 mg/dL** — high.
  - **≥ 190 mg/dL** — very high.

## Interpretation notes

- A single value is a snapshot. The **trajectory over time** (direction, magnitude, and steepest step between consecutive readings) matters more for clinical decisions than any one number.
- A rise in LDL of roughly **45% or more over a year** is a substantial change and should prompt review.
- Threshold used in this platform: **160 mg/dL is flagged as "high"**; crossing it is recorded as a threshold crossing.

## Clinical practice guidance

- LDL trend interpretation should always account for the patient's **genetic risk context** (see APOB / cardiovascular risk note).
- Never issue direct instructions to a patient from a trend alone — frame changes for discussion with the clinician in charge of the patient.
